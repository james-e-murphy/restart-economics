# Results

Written by the evaluator, committed, and small: policy values and margins per configuration, not
derived data. Every file here is regenerated by a command, and no file here existed before the
`plan-frozen` tag.

    ladder.csv      python -m restart.ladder --derived data/derived
                    then python -m restart.fitted --derived data/derived --replicates 1000,
                    which refines the fitted steps' intervals in place. A rerun that adds
                    columns keeps those intervals without repeating restart.fitted:
                    python -m restart.ladder --derived data/derived --fitted-replicates 0
                        --carry results/ladder.csv
                    checks every number both runs computed and carries the intervals over
    breakeven.csv   python -m restart.breakeven --derived data/derived
    cascade.csv     python -m restart.cascade --derived data/derived
    outcome_correlation.csv   written by the same command
    sensitivity_*.csv         python -m restart.sensitivity --derived data/derived
    comparators.csv python -m restart.comparators --derived data/derived
    oracle.csv      python -m restart.oracle --derived data/derived
    difficulty.csv, distribution.csv, spread.csv
                    python -m restart.appendix --derived data/derived
    diagnostics.csv, tail_composition.csv
                    python -m restart.diagnostics --derived data/derived
    coverage.log    python -m restart.coverage
    dynamic_program.log       python -m restart.dynamic

Both runners read `configurations_notes.json`, the audit's own record of which configurations its
exclusion conditions keep out, and score only the configurations it includes. There is no second
list of exclusions to fall out of step with it.

## ladder.csv

One row per configuration, verifier regime and point of the outside-option sweep. The ladder is

| column | step |
|---|---|
| `value_i` | one attempt, no cutoff, then the outside option |
| `value_ii` | *K* attempts, no cutoff |
| `value_iii` | *K* attempts at a constant cutoff |
| `value_iiib` | *K* attempts under a searched schedule of cutoffs |
| `value_iv` | *K* attempts under the state rule |
| `value_iv_transfer` | the same rule, its two models fitted on the other six configurations |

each chosen on training folds and scored on held-out folds, and the margins between neighbouring
rungs are written so that **a positive number is what the richer policy saves**:

    retry_value      = value_i    - value_ii      the value of retrying at all
    cap_margin       = value_ii   - value_iii     the cap's marginal value given retry
    schedule_margin  = value_iii  - value_iiib    the value of varying the cap by attempt
    state_margin     = value_iiib - value_iv      the value of reading the execution's state
    transfer_margin  = value_iiib - value_iv_transfer   the same, read with models from elsewhere

`cap_margin` is the paper's primary result, and `transfer_margin` in the automated regime is its
primary transfer (PLAN.md Section 7). For the transfer, the rule's two models are fitted on the
other six configurations with the held-out fold's tasks excluded from them as well (Section 5),
each configuration given equal weight; the restart values and the attempt budget come from the
target configuration's own training folds, as they do within configuration.

`value_escalate` is **not in the registration** and is labelled so here and in the paper: the cost
of sending every task straight to the outside option without running the agent. The registered
ladder has no policy that declines to run the agent, so where escalation is cheap the constant
cutoff stands in for one by stopping every attempt at five calls. Where `value_iii` sits close to
`value_escalate`, the cap's saving is the saving of not running the agent.

`cap_margin` in more detail: PLAN.md Section 7 writes it in the other order, step
iii minus step ii; the sign here is the one the tables are read in, and the quantity is the same.
`cap_margin_in_sample` is the same comparison without cross-fitting, where the capped family
contains the uncapped one and so cannot lose: a negative figure there would be a bug, and a
negative cross-fitted margin is a finding, the cost of choosing a cutoff from data. It is **not
in the registration** and the paper labels it so where it appears.

`retry_value_low` and `retry_value_high` are the interval on `retry_value`, from the same
bootstrap and the same task resamples as the cap's margin, read together in one pass.

`share_i` to `share_iv_transfer` are, beside each value, the share of tasks that step resolves
without the outside option (PLAN.md Section 3: the agent's own contribution). Each is
cross-fitted like its value: for the policy each fold chose, the mean over the held-out tasks of
the chance, over the orderings of the task's draws, that the policy resolves the task itself.
`tasks_with_draws` counts the tasks with at least one, two, three and four usable draws, which
is what a policy of *K* attempts is estimated on under the all-tasks sensitivity.

`cap_margin_low` and `cap_margin_high` are a 95 percent percentile interval from the
full-pipeline bootstrap at `replicates` replicates, which redoes the split, the selection and the
fitting inside each replicate. Steps iii-b and iv are refitted inside every replicate, so their
intervals are computed only where the plan reads them, in the automated-verifier regime at three
rates spanning the sweep, at the `fitted_replicates` count each row records; elsewhere those
columns are empty and only the point estimates are given. The ladder computes those intervals
from 100 replicates; `restart.fitted` recomputes them at the primary's 1,000, on the same task
resamples (the first 100 of which are the ladder's), from one bootstrap that fits every family
once per replicate, and writes them into this file in place after checking that its point
estimates equal the ladder's. The committed file carries the 1,000-replicate intervals.

Every row carries both axes of the sweep. `rate` is dollars an hour and `multiple` is the same
outside option in multiples of that configuration's `median_attempt_cost`, so that configurations
whose attempts differ manyfold in cost can be read against each other; `axis` says which of the
two grids placed the row. `choice_*` is what each fold chose, and `state_notes` records any
fallback the state rule's two fits had to take.

Values are expected cost per incoming task, in dollars, on the tasks with four usable draws.

## breakeven.csv

The summary statistic of the primary result: the rate at which `cap_margin` changes sign, one row
per crossing, in dollars an hour and in multiples of the median attempt cost. Uniqueness is not
assumed, so the margin is scanned on a logarithmic grid spanning both axes of the sweep, every
bracket where the sign changes is bisected, and all crossings are reported; where the sign never
changes, `crossing` is 0 and `direction` says which way the whole sweep runs. A margin of exactly
zero is its own sign: it is what the comparison returns over the rates where no fold chooses a cap
that binds, and the edges of that range are break-evens. `band_low_rate` and `band_high_rate` are
the rates in `ladder.csv` at which the bootstrap interval still covers zero, which is the range
over which the sign is not resolved.

Each crossing also says whether the ladder resolves the sign on either side of it:
`resolved_below` is true if somewhere between the previous crossing and this one the interval
lies wholly on the side the margin is on, and `resolved_above` likewise up to the next crossing.
A crossing is `supported` when both sides are resolved, that is, when the interval excludes zero
on both sides (the manuscript says "distinct from zero" for this, keeping "resolved" for tasks).
An unsupported crossing is the margin
moving within its own noise; it is reported, as the plan requires, and read as such.

## cascade.csv and outcome_correlation.csv

Step v, on the tasks with four usable draws in every included configuration, one row per regime
and rate on the dollar axis. `value_best_single` is the best single configuration an operator
could have picked from the same training folds, configuration and schedule both chosen there;
`value_cascade` is the best schedule over (configuration, cutoff) pairs, searched from that
single-configuration schedule and so never worse than it on the folds that chose it; and
`switch_margin`, their difference, is the value of switching configurations, positive when the
cascade is cheaper. It has a bootstrap interval at the fitted rates of the automated regime.
`own_<configuration>` is each configuration's own step iii-b on the same tasks, for the figure
that sets the cascade against each configuration's optimum, and `choice_cascade` and
`configurations_used` say what the folds built. At $25, $100 and $300 in the automated regime
and under review at 0.5, `essential_<configuration>` is what the cascade loses when that
configuration is taken out of it (appendix, PLAN.md Section 7). `value_escalate` is the
unregistered escalate-everything reference, as in `ladder.csv`.

`outcome_correlation.csv` is the seven-by-seven correlation, across the same tasks, of each
configuration's share of resolved draws: the algorithm-portfolio predictor of whether a cascade
pays, since switching helps only where failures do not coincide.

## sensitivity_ladder.csv, sensitivity_breakeven.csv, sensitivity_cascade.csv

The registered sensitivities, each changing one input and rerunning the ladder, the break-even
and the cascade under it with every selection redone. The `variant` column names which:

| variant | what changes | registered in PLAN.md |
|---|---|---|
| `phi 0.34`, `phi 0.50` | that share of test-passing attempts are false accepts, each charged the outside option later, and policies re-selected with the charge in the objective | Sections 3 and 10 |
| `common horizon 100` | every configuration at cutoffs up to 95 calls, any attempt still running at 100 stopped there and scored as a failure | Section 10, stopping rules |
| `all tasks` | every task with a usable draw is scored, each policy on the tasks that can fill it, not only the tasks with four usable draws | Section 10, unequal usable draws |
| `refusals excluded` | provider refusals leave the attempt pool | Section 10, the attempt pool |
| `no verdict unresolved` | an evaluation that returned no verdict counts as a failure instead of leaving the pool | Section 10, the attempt pool |
| `re-runs dropped` | executions the harness re-ran after a crash leave the pool | Section 10, the attempt pool |
| `Qwen lowest price` | Qwen3 Coder at $0.22 input and $1.80 output, the lowest third-party price for its weights; every configuration is rescored, since Qwen's attempts are among the rows the others' transfer rules are fitted on | Section 10, price schedules |
| `common tasks` | every configuration scored on the tasks with four usable draws in all seven, the cascade's 275, so that a median across configurations summarizes one task set rather than seven | not registered: a check added after the results were in, marked as such in every exhibit |
| `METR minutes` | the outside option under the bucket-specific correction: METR's measured means for the two buckets it baselined, 32.9 and 131.6 minutes, and between them the correction factor interpolated in logarithms of annotated minutes, 75.1 minutes for the 15-minute-to-1-hour bucket; above 4 hours, where there is nothing to interpolate toward, the factor measured at 1 to 4 hours is held, 526.4 minutes | Section 3 |

The columns are those of `ladder.csv`, `breakeven.csv` and `cascade.csv`, with three differences.
The fitted steps (iii-b, iv and the transfer) are scored on the dollar axis only, as point
estimates; the multiples axis carries steps i to iii, which is what the break-even's resolution
reads. The cap margin's interval is the full-pipeline bootstrap at 1,000 replicates, as in the
primary, computed by a faster route to the same numbers (`restart.sensitivity`, which checks it
against the ladder's point estimate on every row). The cascade is scored as point estimates.

Under `all tasks` each policy is scored on the tasks that can fill it, as the plan registers, so
the two sides of a margin can rest on different tasks: a budget of four attempts is scored only
where four draws are usable, a budget of one wherever one is. Its margins therefore mix a
difference between policies with a difference in the tasks scored; `tasks` is the count for step
i in the ladder and for the chosen cascade in the cascade file, which is why it moves with the
rate there.

## sensitivity_two_stage.csv

The interval sensitivity of PLAN.md Section 5: a two-stage bootstrap that resamples each task's
usable draws with replacement as well as the tasks. For the cap's margin (`margin` = `cap`) it is
computed at every point of the sweep in every regime, beside the one-stage interval, which is the
interval in `ladder.csv`. Both use the same task resamples, drawn from the primary's seed, so the
two intervals differ only by the second stage. For the primary transfer (`margin` = `transfer`) it
is computed where `ladder.csv` gives that margin an interval, the automated regime at $25, $100
and $300, at the same count and on the same task resamples; the one-stage interval to set it
beside is `transfer_margin_low` and `transfer_margin_high` there. `estimate` is the point estimate,
and `one_stage_mean` and `two_stage_mean` are where each bootstrap's replicates are centred.

A draw drawn twice is replayed as two attempts. For a mean over tasks, resampling at both stages
counts the within-task variation twice, so its interval is the wider. Policy value is not such a
mean: a retry draws a task's attempts without replacement, so a replicate whose task holds one draw
twice prices a retry that can meet the same attempt again, and the value of retrying, and of
capping the retries, moves with it. The two-stage replicates therefore need not be centred where
the one-stage ones are, and the two centres are given so that a shift of the interval can be told
from a widening of it. Neither is corrected.

## comparators.csv

The comparators PLAN.md registers beside the ladder, each its own family chosen on the training
folds and scored on the held-out folds, on the tasks with four usable draws, one row per
configuration, regime and point of the sweep. Reported in full and not argued from.

| column | family |
|---|---|
| `value_dollar` | *K* attempts, each stopped at the first decision point at which its spend exceeds a dollar cutoff: 40 cutoffs log-spaced between the 5th percentile of spend at call 5 and the largest attempt spend on the training folds |
| `value_threshold` | the two-parameter threshold comparator: stop at call *t* when spend per call over the last five calls exceeds *a* + *b t*, with *a* at the 5th to 95th percentiles of that rate over running attempts on the training folds and *b* moving the threshold at the last decision point from *a*/4 to 4*a* |
| `value_universal` | the universal restart schedule, cutoffs in the sequence 1, 1, 2, 1 of a unit chosen on the training folds from the decision points whose double is also one (or at or past the stop); the plan does not fix the unit, and a unit of one call would stop every attempt at once |
| `value_iv_first` | the state rule allowed to act only at the first decision point, with the same two models |

beside the ladder's own steps on the same folds (`value_ii`, `value_iii`, `value_iiib`,
`value_iv`). The margins are written so that positive is what the richer or registered policy
saves: `dollar_cap_margin` (step ii minus the dollar cutoff, the primary result with the cap in
dollars), `dollar_vs_calls`, `rule_vs_threshold` (the threshold minus the state rule: if the
regressions add nothing it is not positive), `threshold_vs_schedule`, `schedule_vs_universal` (the
universal schedule minus step iii-b), `universal_vs_retry`, `state_margin` and
`first_look_margin` (step iii-b minus the first-look rule). The four registered margins have
intervals where the ladder's fitted steps do, in the automated regime at $25, $100 and $300, from
100 replicates with every family refitted inside each (`fitted_replicates`).

## oracle.csv

The sample oracle benchmark on the cascade's common tasks: for each task the cheapest schedule
of one to four (configuration, cutoff) attempts on that task's own draws, averaged over tasks
(`value_oracle`), beside the best cross-fitted cascade and single configuration on the same tasks.
The minimum is exact, found by a recursion the tests hold to a brute-force search over every
schedule. It bounds the evaluated class but is biased downward, a minimum over configurations
taken on four draws per task, so `oracle_gap`, the cascade minus the oracle, is an upper estimate
of what knowing the task in advance would be worth.

## difficulty.csv, distribution.csv, spread.csv

`difficulty.csv` is the cap's marginal value within each bucket of the benchmark's difficulty
annotation, every policy chosen within the bucket, with a 1,000-replicate interval. Each of the
four annotated buckets has its rows, and the two longest are also shown together as `1 hour or
more`, because the longest holds a handful of tasks. A bucket with fewer than two scored tasks
per fold is too small for a training fold to choose on, and its rows carry the count of tasks and
no value. The rows `all, chosen within bucket` pool the choices made under 15 minutes, from 15
minutes to 1 hour and at 1 hour or more, and `all, chosen blind` is the primary comparison, so
their difference is what admitting the annotation to every policy is worth. `multiple` is on the
configuration's whole scored set.

`distribution.csv` gives, for steps i to iii-b as chosen on mean cost, the mean (`value_*`),
median and 95th percentile of cost per incoming task, taken over tasks and the orderings of their
draws with every task weighing the same, as lower quantiles; `cap_margin_p95` is the cap's saving
at the 95th percentile. `cap_margin_median` is the median-cost version of the primary comparison:
steps ii and iii each chosen on training folds by median cost and scored by the median of the
held-out costs.

`spread.csv` is, on the cascade's common tasks, the range across configurations of their best
policy's value (`across_best`) and of a single attempt's (`across_one`), against the range across
steps i to iii-b within a configuration (`within_median`, `within_max`).

## diagnostics.csv, tail_composition.csv

Per configuration on its tasks with four usable draws: the share of the variance of log spend
that lies within tasks, the shares of tasks whose four draws are mixed, all fail and all resolve,
and the share of attempts whose spend passes their own task's outside option at $25 and $100 an
hour. `tail_composition.csv` gives, at each decision point, the share of attempts still running,
the share of all spend incurred after it, the share of that later spend incurred by attempts that
go on to resolve, the chance a running attempt resolves, and the share of running attempts whose
spend has already passed their task's outside option.

## coverage.log

The verification of the primary result's interval (PLAN.md Section 8), on a synthetic population
with a known answer. What the cross-fitted margin estimates is the population value of the
policies the procedure chooses from a training set; the log gives, at four settings, that target,
the best policy in the population beside it, the bias of the point estimate for the target, and
how often the registered 95 percent interval covers it over 120 samples of 500 tasks.

## dynamic_program.log

The other verification PLAN.md Section 8 asks of the synthetic dry run: which way and by how much
the receding-horizon rule departs from the exact dynamic program, where that is computable. The
attempts are simulated from a law whose state is the rule's own, so the optimum over the rule's
state and actions is a backward induction and exact, and its own policy, replayed by the
evaluator on the simulated attempts, reproduces its value (`replayed`). On 8,000 held-out
simulated tasks the log sets beside it the state rule fitted on another 2,000, the best constant
cutoff and retrying without a cap, and says what share of first attempts the optimum
and the rule each stop before their end, and at how many calls on average. Tasks are alike in
this population, so the rule's restart values being averages over tasks is not tested here.
